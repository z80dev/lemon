# Architecture Review, September 2026

Snapshot of the tree at `86aa731` (2026-09-01). Counts come from `grep`,
`wc`, and `git`; nothing here was compiled. Where a claim needs `mix xref` to
be exact, the review says so.

## 1. Verdict

Lemon is not lost because the Elixir is bad. Module docs are unusually
careful, spec coverage is decent, credo runs at zero violations, boundaries
are checked in CI, and the extension behaviours (`LemonChannels.Plugin`,
`LemonAi.Provider`, `LemonMemory.Provider`, `LemonCore.Store.Backend`,
`LemonCore.EngineRuntime`) are the right idea, backed by a real contract-test
kit.

It is lost because it grew by accretion. The history was reset on 2026-08-18
and 330 commits landed in the following two weeks, most of them integrated
from parallel agent worktrees. Each worktree added its corner correctly and
documented it thoroughly. Nobody was deleting. The result is a 25-app umbrella
holding two products and a platform, where the dependency graph in `mix.exs`
is clean and the real graph, wired through `config.exs`, application env,
dynamic module atoms, and runtime reflection, is not.

Three problems account for most of the damage:

1. **The layering is defended by lint instead of design.** The boundary
   checker has grown to roughly thirty substring bans of the form "X must go
   through wrapper Y". When a rule is needed to stop two modules from
   re-merging, they are one module.
2. **Storage is a generic key-value bag with 77 implicit schemas.** Every
   domain either adds functions to `LemonCore.Store` or wraps it in a
   fifteen-line `*Store` module and then adds a ban so nobody bypasses the
   wrapper.
3. **One conversation turn crosses six processes in three apps and is
   re-encoded through four event vocabularies.** The router and the gateway
   both claim, in their own moduledocs, to own the run lifecycle.

Everything else in this review is a consequence or a symptom of those three.

## 3. Findings

Each finding states what was observed, why it matters, and what to do. They
are ordered by leverage, not severity.

### 3.1 Two products and a platform share one umbrella, and the split is a month overdue

`config/config.exs` and `config/runtime.exs` carry three fenced blocks
labelled "lemon-sim product block, moves to the lemon-sim repo". The
[platform split plan](../platform-split.md) (v2, 2026-08-09) and the
[extraction readiness doc](../platform/phase-5-extraction.md) (2026-08-10)
both conclude that lemon-sim is ready to leave now, with no unpublished
dependencies. It is still here.

Why it matters: every reader, every `mix compile`, every `mix test`, and
every architecture rule pays for 131K lines that the platform does not need
and that `docs/platform-split.md` already decided belong elsewhere.

Do: extract `lemon_sim`, `lemon_sim_ui`, `lemon_tcg`, `showcase/`,
the sim mix aliases, the three config blocks, and the `LemonSimUi.Env` and
`LemonTcg.Env` registry entries with `git filter-repo`, exactly as the plan
describes. This is mechanical and it is the single largest legibility win
available.

### 3.2 The dependency graph on paper is not the dependency graph at runtime

`lemon_core` declares zero umbrella dependencies. In its `lib/` it also
contains:

- five bridge modules (`RouterBridge`, `EngineRuntime`, `EngineInfoBridge`,
  `EventBridge`, `ACPClientBridge`) whose purpose is to call upward into
  router, gateway, and control plane without naming them;
- `LemonCore.GatewayConfig`, which reads
  `Application.get_env(:lemon_gateway, :"Elixir.LemonGateway.Config")`;
- `Mix.Tasks.Lemon.Update`, which hardcodes
  `:"Elixir.LemonSkills.BuiltinSeeder"` and `:"Elixir.LemonSkills.Migrator"`;
- about 70 `Code.ensure_loaded?` / `function_exported?` sites, of which
  perhaps a dozen are legitimate optional-dependency probes (Exqlite,
  Phoenix.PubSub, FileSystem, Sentry).

The same pattern repeats one layer up. `LemonChannels.Runtime.cancel_by_run_id/2`
first tries `LemonCore.RouterBridge.abort_run/2`, then falls back to
`apply(:"Elixir.LemonRouter.Router", :abort_run, ...)`. Two soft paths to the
same function. `lemon_channels` has 75 reflection sites, `lemon_control_plane`
45, `coding_agent` 43.

`config/config.exs` then completes the hidden graph. Eleven injection points
(`:engine_runtime`, `:executor`, `:workspace_dir`, `:doctor_runtime`,
`:doctor_checks`, `:gateway_channels`, `:env_registries`,
`:workspace_diagnostics`, `:runtime_endpoints`, `:finalize_run_hooks`,
`:run_history_provider`) name about 45 modules from a dozen apps. The rule
"the platform never names a product" is honoured by moving the naming into
config, where no tool checks it.

Why it matters: `mix lemon.quality` proves that `mix.exs` matches a policy
table. It does not prove that `lemon_core` can boot, compile, or be published
without the apps above it. The policy table has a
`reference_only_namespace_exceptions` escape hatch precisely because the
gateway needs `LemonAi` structs and `LemonAutomation` without declaring them.

Do:

- Keep exactly three inversion seams and make each a named behaviour with a
  single registration call verified at configure time (`Code.ensure_loaded!`
  plus a behaviour check), so no call site needs `function_exported?`:
  - `LemonCore.Executor`: how a run is executed (today `EngineRuntime` plus
    `LemonGateway.Executor`, two behaviours for one seam).
  - `LemonCore.Inbox`: how an adapter submits and aborts work (today
    `RouterBridge`, plus the dynamic-atom fallbacks that must go).
  - `LemonCore.Doctor.Check` and `LemonCore.Env.Registry`: the two
    registries that genuinely need many contributors.
- Delete `EngineInfoBridge`, `LemonCore.GatewayConfig`, and every
  `:"Elixir.X"` atom in `lib/`. Where a lower app needs something a higher
  app owns, the thing is in the wrong app.
- Replace the substring dependency check with `mix xref graph --format
  stats --label compile-connected` per app, which sees aliases, remote calls,
  and dynamic atoms that the checker's own AST pass already tries to
  emulate.

### 3.3 Storage: one generic bag, 77 tables, 48 wrappers, 36 bans

`LemonCore.Store` is 1,781 lines with 70 public functions. Its moduledoc says
it "deliberately knows nothing about run history, memory, chat platforms, or
any other domain". It then exposes chat state with TTL sweeps, run event
append and finalize hooks, progress-message-id to run-id mapping (a Telegram
concept), four policy tables, and introspection events with retention, on top
of the generic `get/put/put_new/delete/list/take/compare_and_swap`.

Around it, 48 `*_store.ex` modules exist mostly to give one table a name.
`LemonControlPlane.TtsStore` is fifteen lines wrapping
`Store.get(:tts_config, :global)`. The control plane has thirteen of these,
`lemon_core` fourteen, `coding_agent` nine. Seventy-seven distinct tables are
named in generic `Store` calls across the umbrella, most of them through a
`@table` attribute inside a wrapper. Then
`LemonCore.Quality.ArchitectureRulesCheck` carries 36 rules, most of them with
patterns are literal strings like `LemonCore.Store.get(:model_policies`, each
banning a bypass of one wrapper in one directory.

Why it matters: this is a type system implemented as grep. It cannot enumerate
the tables that exist, cannot migrate them, cannot give a table its own
backend or retention, and every new table costs a wrapper plus a rule. The
platform split evidence (E1 in `docs/platform-split.md`) already lists the
same symptoms.

Do:

- Add `LemonCore.Store.Table`. A table is declared once, in the app that owns
  it:

  ```elixir
  defmodule LemonControlPlane.TtsConfig do
    use LemonCore.Store.Table, name: :tts_config, key: :global, value: map()
  end
  ```

  The macro generates `get/1`, `put/2`, `delete/1`, `list/0` with specs,
  registers the table so `Store` can list and migrate it, and is the only
  place allowed to call the generic API. One quality rule replaces thirty:
  generic `Store` calls may appear only inside a `Store.Table` module.
- Move the domain APIs out of `Store` into tables owned by the domain: chat
  state and progress mapping to `lemon_channels`, policies to `lemon_router`,
  introspection to its own module, run events to `lemon_router`. `Store`
  becomes a backend-dispatching GenServer plus the read cache, well under
  500 lines.
- Retire the wrapper modules as tables are declared; the file count is the
  progress meter.

### 3.4 The run pipeline: six processes, four event vocabularies, two owners

A single inbound message traverses, in order:

| Hop | App | Process | Lines |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `SessionCoordinator` (queue semantics) | lemon_router | per conversation | 837 |
| `RunProcess` + 5 submodules ("owns a single run lifecycle") | lemon_router | per run | 1,057 |
| `EngineRuntime` behaviour → `LemonGateway.Runtime` | lemon_core → lemon_gateway | | 174 + 74 |
| `Scheduler` (slots) → `ThreadWorker` (per-conversation FIFO) | lemon_gateway | per conversation | 706 + 439 |
| `Run` ("managing run lifecycle: start, steer, cancel, complete") | lemon_gateway | per run | 958 |
| `Executor` behaviour → `CodingAgent.Executor` → `SessionRunner` | lemon_gateway → coding_agent | | 79 + 176 |
| `Session` + 17 submodules, 65-field state | coding_agent | per session | 2,110 + 6,476 |
| `Agent` → `Loop` | lemon_agent | per session | 1,328 + 878 |

Events come back through `LemonAgent` loop tuples, then `{:session_event}`,
then `{:engine_event}` / `{:engine_delta}`, then `LemonCore.Event` on the
`run:<id>` bus topic, which `RunProcess` re-broadcasts on `session:<key>`,
then `SurfaceManager` and `StreamCoalescer`, then `DeliveryIntent`, then
`OutboundPayload`. `CodingAgent.Session.RunTranslator` is 370 lines of
translation between two of those vocabularies, and `LemonCore.Events` has 26
structs.

The router/gateway boundary is held in place by eight dedicated lint rules
(`gateway_execution_queue_mode`, `gateway_legacy_runtime_submit`,
`gateway_conversation_key_selection`, `gateway_auto_resume_mutation`,
`gateway_job_compatibility`, `router_gateway_cwd_dependency`, and the two
router session-registry rules). Both apps have a `RunSupervisor`, a
`BindingResolver`, a `Cwd`, a `Health` router, and a `Config`.

Of `lemon_gateway`'s 11,967 lines, about 6,200 (`sms/`, `voice/`,
`transports/`, `tools/`) are ingress that the project's own transport
unification doc calls dead by default, behind a second transport behaviour
(`LemonGateway.Transport`, three callbacks) that duplicates
`LemonChannels.Plugin` (six callbacks). `coding_agent` depends on
`lemon_gateway` for seven `LemonGateway.Tools.*` tools (SMS, Telegram image,
Discord file, cron), which are product tools living inside an execution
scheduler.

Why it matters: this is where new features go to get complicated. Every
run-level behaviour (steer, cancel, resume, compaction, watchdogs) must be
threaded through two lifecycle owners and four encodings, and tested at each.
It is also why `run_process_test.exs` is 3,399 lines and
`apps/lemon_gateway/test/run_test.exs` is 2,882.

The platform split plan's decision D1 kept router and gateway separate because
"router↔gateway is already a published-package-quality boundary". The
boundary that has package quality is `EngineRuntime`, the executor seam, and
it survives a merge. There is no plausible third-party consumer of a scheduler
without the router that feeds it.

Do:

- Fold the gateway's execution half (`Scheduler`, `ThreadWorker`, `Run`,
  `RunSupervisor`, `EngineLock`, `RunRegistry`) into `lemon_router` as
  `LemonRouter.Execution.*`. One lifecycle owner, one `RunSupervisor`.
- Collapse `EngineRuntime` and `LemonGateway.Executor` into one
  `LemonCore.Executor` behaviour (`submit/1`, `cancel/2`, `steer/2`,
  `available?/0`) implemented by `CodingAgent.Executor`.
- Have `CodingAgent.Session` emit `LemonCore.Event` structs directly; delete
  `RunTranslator` and the `{:engine_event}` layer. Target: three processes
  per turn (coordinator, run, agent) and one event vocabulary from executor
  to channel.
- Move the seven gateway tools next to the media tools in
  `lemon_skills/tools`. Move SMS, voice, and webhook ingress into satellites
  on the `x_api` model, or delete what has no user. Delete
  `LemonGateway.Transport`.
- Delete the eight lint rules as the code they guard disappears.

### 3.5 The same concept implemented three times

| Concept | Copies | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Binding` struct | 3 | `LemonCore.Binding`, `LemonChannels.Binding`, `LemonGateway.Binding` are field-for-field identical |
| `BindingResolver` | 3 | channels (119 lines) and gateway (133 lines) exist only to convert to core types, call the core resolver (196 lines), and convert back |
| `Cwd` | 3 | `LemonChannels.Cwd` is an 8-line alias; `LemonGateway.Cwd` re-implements core with `Process.whereis` |
| `[gateway]` TOML accessor | 4 | `LemonCore.GatewayConfig`, `LemonGateway.Config` (a GenServer), `LemonGateway.ConfigLoader`, `LemonChannels.GatewayConfig` via `EngineInfoBridge` |
| Config loader layers | 3 | `LemonCore.Config` → `ConfigCache` → `Config.Modular`, each described as canonical somewhere |
| `Health` HTTP router | 4 | gateway, router, control plane, plus per-endpoint health |
| `Checkpoint` | 3 | core, coding_agent, coding_agent tool |
| `Registry` modules | 11 | |
| Modules named `*Config*` | 25 | |

Do: one `Binding`, one resolver, one `Cwd`, one `LemonCore.Config.gateway/0`.
Delete the GenServer that caches a TOML section and the bridge that reads it
back. Each deletion also removes a lint rule or a reflection site.

### 3.6 Swallow-everything error handling has become the house style

There are 1,228 `rescue` clauses and 411 `catch` clauses in `lib/`. The
Discord transport alone has 35 bare `rescue` clauses. Many wrap a single store call
(`safe_delete_chat_state/1`, `clear_cwd_override/1`) or a `start_link`
(`safe_start_consumer/0`).

The bridges document this as a deliberate contract: "every function here
answers rather than raises". That is correct at a process boundary. It has
spread into ordinary domain functions, where it hides bugs from supervisors,
from Dialyzer, and from the person reading the code.

Do: adopt and enforce a two-line rule. A `rescue` is allowed only at a
process boundary (adapter inbound handler, bridge call, tool execution,
scheduled job body), and it must name the exception it expects. Add a ratchet
to `mix lemon.quality` so the count can only go down.

### 3.7 Tests are large, coupled, and clocked by `Process.sleep`

About 1,240 `Process.sleep` calls in tests (360 of them `sleep(50)`, 199
`sleep(10)`), while
[deterministic-test-patterns.md](../testing/deterministic-test-patterns.md)
says to prefer explicit synchronization and `LemonCore.Testing.AsyncHelpers`
exists for the purpose. 590 of 1,027 test files are not async.
`apps/coding_agent/test/test_helper.exs` rewrites `HOME`, `require_file`s
support modules from sibling apps by relative path, and restarts applications
because "earlier app suites may have stopped :coding_agent". Forty of the 80
files in `scripts/` are `live_*_smoke` and `live_*_proof` scripts: a second,
unstructured test layer.

Do: replace every `sleep` with `assert_receive`, `AsyncHelpers`, or a
monitor; make the suite async by default and mark the exceptions; move
shared test support into `lemon_platform_test` where it already has a home;
fold the live scripts into `@tag :integration` tests or delete them.

### 3.8 God modules and one 65-field struct

Seventy-one library files exceed 1,000 lines. The ones that matter are not
data catalogs (the OpenRouter and Vercel model tables should simply become
`priv/*.json`), they are the ones with behaviour:

| File | Lines |
| --- | --- |
| `lemon_channels/adapters/discord/transport.ex` | 3,095 |
| `coding_agent/session.ex` (plus 17 submodules, 6,476 lines) | 2,110 |
| `coding_agent/tools/browser.ex` | 1,899 |
| `lemon_automation/cron_manager.ex` | 1,825 |
| `lemon_core/store.ex` | 1,781 |
| `lemon_skills/mcp_source.ex` | 1,712 |
| `coding_agent/tools/agent.ex` | 1,683 |
| `lemon_control_plane/protocol/schemas.ex` | 1,623 |

`CodingAgent.Session`'s state has 65 fields. Six are heartbeat timers, five
are auto-compaction task bookkeeping, eight are overflow-recovery bookkeeping.
Those are three state machines sharing one struct even though `session/`
already has `heartbeat.ex`, `compaction_lifecycle.ex`, and
`overflow_recovery.ex`.

Do: give each submodule its own struct and a single field in `Session`.
Split the Discord transport by concern (gateway events, slash commands,
components, delivery). Extract the control-plane method schemas into
per-method modules that declare their own schema, so `schemas.ex` and the
3,071-line parity test stop being the two most-churned files in the repo.

### 3.9 Documentation outpaces maintenance

191 markdown files. Per-app `AGENTS.md` files total 493 KB; `coding_agent`'s
alone is 66 KB and the control plane's 63 KB. The root `AGENTS.md` mixes
architecture with agent-team process (model tiers, worktree conventions).
`SPEC.md` at the repo root is a single-feature task spec containing a
personal worktree path and "fix round" notes. `.credo.exs` and
`.dialyzer_ignore.exs` both cite `docs/plans/`, which does not exist.
`docs/architecture/overview.md` still uses the old app names (`ai`,
`agent_core`) and says "112+ RPC methods"; there are 172 method modules.

The docs catalog with owner and freshness metadata is a good mechanism, and
`mix lemon.quality` enforces it. Date-based freshness does not catch the
semantic drift above.

Do: delete `SPEC.md`; move process guidance out of `AGENTS.md` into
`CONTRIBUTING.md`; cap each app's `AGENTS.md` at roughly 5 KB covering
purpose, boundaries, entry points, and invariants, with everything else in
moduledocs where it is next to the code it describes; fix the stale
references.

### 3.10 What is genuinely good, and should be protected

- `lemon_ai`: 32K lines, zero umbrella dependencies, 72 test files. A real
  library.
- The behaviours with written contracts and `lemon_platform_test` case
  templates. This is the extension model; more of the tree should be built on
  it.
- The `x_api` satellite pattern: depends on the platform, registers itself at
  boot, nothing names it. This should be how every vendor channel adapter
  works.
- `LemonCore.Env.Registry`: env vars declared as data next to the app that
  reads them, rendered into `docs/config-registry.md`.
- The publishing mechanics (`hex_package.exs`), the Elixir 1.15 floor lane,
  warnings-as-errors, no skipped tests, zero credo violations, the Dialyzer
  lane with a reasoned ignore file.
- Moduledocs that explain failure contracts and rejected alternatives
  (`RouterBridge`, `ACPClientBridge`, `EngineRuntime`).

## 4. The plan

Phases are ordered by leverage divided by risk. Each has an exit criterion
that `mix lemon.quality` can measure; sizes are relative. This is the original
proposal: section 7 revises the order and several prescriptions after a second
review, and section 8 tracks progress against the revised order.

### Phase 0: stop the accretion (size S)

- Add ratchets to `mix lemon.quality` for the counts in section 5. A ratchet
  fails when a count rises above the committed baseline and lowers the
  baseline when a PR reduces it.
- Delete `SPEC.md`. Move process guidance out of `AGENTS.md`. Fix the
  `docs/plans` references and the stale numbers in
  `docs/architecture/overview.md`.
- Exit: ratchets green on `main`; no working artifacts at the repo root.

### Phase 1: extract lemon-sim (size M, mechanical)

- Execute the extraction that `docs/platform/phase-5-extraction.md` already
  declares ready.
- Exit: umbrella at 22 apps and about 340K library lines; no "product block"
  fences in `config/`.

### Phase 2: typed, owned storage (size M)

- `LemonCore.Store.Table`; declare the 77 tables in their owning apps; move
  the domain APIs out of `Store`; retire wrappers; replace the storage rules
  with one.
- Exit: `Store` under 500 lines; zero `*_store.ex` wrappers that only alias a
  table; one storage rule in `ArchitectureRulesCheck`.

### Phase 3: one run pipeline (size L, the real work)

- Fold gateway execution into `LemonRouter.Execution`; one `LemonCore.Executor`
  behaviour; `CodingAgent.Session` emits `LemonCore.Event` directly; gateway
  tools to `lemon_skills`; ingress to satellites; delete
  `LemonGateway.Transport`.
- Exit: three processes per turn; `RunTranslator` gone; `{:engine_event}`
  gone; eight router/gateway rules gone; `run_test.exs` and
  `run_process_test.exs` merged into one suite under 2,000 lines.

### Phase 4: dissolve the bridges and the duplicates (size M)

- One `Binding`, one resolver, one `Cwd`, one gateway config accessor;
  `EngineInfoBridge`, `LemonCore.GatewayConfig`, `LemonGateway.Config`
  deleted; `RouterBridge` becomes `LemonCore.Inbox` with a verified
  implementation and no fallbacks.
- Move the Telegram adapter into a satellite app first, because it is the
  adapter whose concepts leak into router and core; the five `telegram_*`
  and `core_vendor_channel_reference` rules become compile errors instead.
- Exit: zero `:"Elixir.` atoms in `lib/`; reflection sites under 40, all
  optional-dependency probes; `mix xref graph` shows no edge from
  `lemon_core` to any other umbrella app.

### Phase 5: code-quality ratchets, ongoing

- The `rescue` policy; `sleep` to `AsyncHelpers`; `Session` state split;
  model catalogs to `priv/`; Discord transport and control-plane schemas
  split; `AGENTS.md` caps.

## 6. What this review did not verify

- No Elixir toolchain was available in the review environment, so nothing
  was compiled and neither `mix lemon.quality` nor the test suite was run.
  The reflection, rescue, and sleep counts are pattern counts and include a
  small number of comments and doc examples. The first draft of this review
  counted 44 generic store tables; resolving `@table` attributes per file,
  as the ratchet now does, gives 77.
- The run pipeline hop table was assembled from moduledocs and call sites,
  not from a trace. `mix xref graph --label compile-connected` from each
  app's directory is the authoritative check for section 3.2.
- Effort sizes are relative. The phases are sequenced so that each one makes
  the next one smaller.

## 7. Revisions after review, 2026-09-01

A second reviewer read this document against the same snapshot and
challenged several of its prescriptions. The points below supersede the
sections they name. The findings stand; the fixes change.

- **Truthful failures first (3.2, 3.6).** The highest-value fix is making
  failure observable. `LemonChannels.Runtime.cancel_by_run_id/2` and the
  bridges answer `:ok` whether the work was done, no implementation exists,
  or the implementation raised, so an expected no-op, a missing seam and a
  bug are indistinguishable to the caller and invisible to supervisors. The
  rescue rule becomes: a failure is observable, the caller receives an
  accurate outcome, and continuing leaves valid state. Plugin and tool
  boundaries may catch any exception as long as they report it. Counting
  `rescue` clauses is a signal, not a target.
- **Config injection is composition (3.2).** Choosing an implementation of a
  documented contract in `config.exs` is how the seams are meant to be
  composed. What must go is lower-level code naming higher-level modules
  through dynamic atoms and substituting behaviour when they are missing.
  Each seam keeps its behaviour and validates the configured implementation
  at configure time, so call sites need no `function_exported?` guard. The
  claim that a boundary needing a rule is not a boundary is withdrawn.
  `mix xref` is one signal beside standalone package compiles and runtime
  contract tests, not the authority.
- **No router and gateway merge (3.4).** `EngineRuntime` (accept a command,
  report availability, cancel by run id, expose a pid to monitor) and
  `LemonGateway.Executor` (start an attempt against an event sink, cancel,
  steer, redirect) are different contracts at different levels: controller,
  execution host, agent. An execution-only host driven by another Lemon node
  is a real consumer of the second without the first, which is the named
  execution-node direction the project already has. The problem to solve is
  two components claiming one lifecycle. Phase 4 therefore writes down
  ownership for the logical run, the execution attempt and the agent
  session, with one owner per transition, timeout, retry and terminal
  result, and removes only the overlap that tests or traces demonstrate.
  Process count per turn and test-file length are no longer exit criteria.
  Each process is judged on what it exclusively owns, what it serializes,
  its lifetime, and what should happen when it crashes.
- **Define the event contract, keep the behaviour (3.4).** `RunTranslator`
  tracks pending tool actions, approvals, streamed text and usage. Deleting
  it does not delete that work. The goal is a public execution-event
  contract with redundant representations removed, while agent-internal
  events and delivery payloads may legitimately differ.
- **Storage moves incrementally (3.3).** Domain behaviour leaves `Store`
  first, into small domain modules that own meaning, validation, defaults and
  retention, over a storage API that owns backend access, atomic operations
  and cache coherence. Small domain modules are not the problem. Table
  registration is trialled on two or three different domains before wider
  adoption, with explicit answers on schema versioning, missing versus
  failed reads, atomic update, retention and backend routing, and the
  documented cache-coherence and sync/async semantics are preserved. The
  measure is generic `Store` calls outside owning modules, not the number of
  wrapper modules.
- **Tests: global state before async (3.7).** The `HOME` rewrite, the
  cross-app `require_file` calls and the application restarts in the
  coding-agent helper are the coupling to remove. More `async: true` follows
  from that, not the other way round.
- **Sim extraction is a packaging decision (3.1).** It reduces this
  repository, not the complexity maintained across the project, and nothing
  later depends on it. It stays first because the project's own plan already
  decided it and the work is mechanical, and it is reversible until merged.

Revised order: Phase 0 ratchets, Phase 1 sim extraction, Phase 2 reliability
contracts, Phase 3 owned storage, Phase 4 run ownership and the event
contract, Phase 5 duplicates and bridges, Phase 6 code quality. The ratchets
in section 5 are no-regression guards: they keep counts from rising while the
work lowers them, and the target column is direction, not a definition of
done.
